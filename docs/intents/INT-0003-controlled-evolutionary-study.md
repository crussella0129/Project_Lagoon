## Intent

Test whether voluntary model partner choice changes descendant capabilities,
behavior, diversity, and lineage structure relative to explicit controls across
multiple generations. Observe possible specialties and families without scripting
social class, human stereotypes, or elimination incentives.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1:** compare self-selection, random compatible pairing, and a benchmark-
  based pairing control under matched model pools, inference/merge budgets,
  exposure opportunities, merger settings, and held-out evaluation.
- **AC-2:** repeat across recorded seeds and report uncertainty, abstention,
  reciprocity, admission failures, capability profiles, and lineage concentration.
  Do not infer Nash equilibria without a defined game, payoff model, and analysis.
- **AC-3:** distinguish memory-only changes from weight changes. Any explicit
  learning/training mechanism has separate provenance and an approved protocol.
- **AC-4:** leave parents alive and specify a bounded admission/waitlist policy
  for descendants before running a growing population. Explain the consequences
  of limited attention and compute for which participants receive exposure.
- **AC-5:** treat universal abstention or declining reciprocity as valid outcomes.
  Any loneliness treatment is explicitly declared, separately compared against
  the unpressured baseline, and never retroactively presented as neutral choice.

## Rationale

Evolutionary merging already exists; the specific hypothesis is whether partner
choice carries useful information about complementarity or yields interesting
stable behavior. Transcripts, self-reports, merge quality, and actual inheritance
require distinct measurements.

## Alternatives

A narrative demo may be entertaining but cannot establish the evolutionary
hypothesis. Benchmark-only optimization is a useful control rather than the
project's sole selection mechanism.

## Consequences

Requires INT-0001 and INT-0002 plus a bounded experimental design. Novelty,
emergent specialties, and better alignment remain hypotheses. No automatic
fine-tuning or participant distress mechanism is included by default.
